# Ship camera and attitude instrument

The main Orbitalis client offers ship observation whenever optional Sable is installed. It works
on a native ship deck and on a plot-owned passenger seat. It does not require the flight consumer,
Create or Propulsion in the production dependency graph.

Press **F6** to toggle the external camera. Mouse movement orbits the hull without changing the
player's look; the wheel adjusts distance from one to eight times the fitted view. Bindings appear
under Orbitalis in Controls. The initial distance fits the hull's bounding sphere to the actual
render FOV and window aspect, including portrait windows, rotated hulls and native scaling. Terrain
can bring the camera closer to avoid clipping; a nearby obstruction can therefore hide part of the
ship. The observation camera stays world-upright during ship rolls and retrograde turns.
Press F6 again to restore the previous perspective. F5 deliberately leaves observation mode. Death,
disconnect and sustained loss of ship contact also leave it. During a handoff the camera only resumes
on the same ship UUID after native contact returns; it never chooses a nearby replacement ship.

The navball includes pitch lines every 15 degrees, stronger lines and labels at 30-degree intervals,
heading meridians and a graduated bank rim. Its grid follows the same local gravity frame as the
horizon, including attitudes that fill the disk with one hemisphere.

The circular navball appears while aboard a ship in the Overworld/space flight route. Blue is above
the local gravity horizon, brown below; the fixed white centre marks the nose. Its attitude follows
the ship, independent of the external camera. Yellow marks prograde; orange marks retrograde on the
visible hemisphere. Markers disappear below 0.25 m/s or when fresh native movement samples are absent.
The speed is estimated from consecutive native server pose samples, never from player/seat position.
The gravity direction uses the same finite-body field factory as construction forces; an arbitrary
unit acceleration changes no direction and requires no independent client mass model or network data.

Default nose is local **+Y** for the vertical rocket fixture, with screen-up **-Z** and right **+X**.
Press **F7** to switch to local **+Z** for a horizontal ship, with screen-up **+Y** and right **+X**.
The displayed axis makes this choice explicit; it changes the instrument only, never physics or guidance.
F1 hides the instrument with the rest of the HUD. Menus suppress it and leave normal scrolling alone.
No external textures or KSP artwork are imported: the horizon is generated by original client code.

Canonical framing and instrument geometry live in shared/common. NeoForge owns client registration
and the narrow camera/input mixins. Their optional hooks do nothing without the Sable adapter; no
Sable/client classes are introduced into the core or server registration path. Native transforms,
clipping, interpolation, entity mounting and transfer journals remain authoritative.

## Verification scenario

In a disposable consumer Overworld, create and assemble the rocket using the documented flight
functions. Board its deck or place a Create seat in its cabin and sit. Test F6, mouse orbit, wheel zoom,
F6 restoration, F5 exit and Controls rebinding. Resize landscape/portrait and vary GUI scale. Check F1
and menu input. Fly a complete orbit and return: the camera must reacquire the same ship after both
handoffs, the horizon must follow the hull through the RCS turn, and velocity markers must reverse
relative to the nose when retrograde. Repeat without Sable to check optional client loading.
Automated native transfer tests do not establish appearance or real-client passenger handshakes.
